# AIN capacity repair proposals

Proposed sequence as of 6 October 2026: A.1 determines which spare days are legal, A.2 publishes capacity only for those choices, and B reduces that valid spare further when a Resource exceeds its cap. Spare that breaks the five-worked-day limit or required two-day break must be excluded before B's further reductions. Final checks verify that rule; they are not a substitute for upstream eligibility.

## Problem origins

A **recent change** finding is established by a recorded revision. These categories identify where the code problem originated; they do not establish which problem caused a particular saved BD result.

## Proposed changes in dependency order

1. **Restore complete allocation coverage. Source: recent change.** In A.1 `A1ResourceIdentity_CHECK`, replace the forced allocation-coverage `Pass` and zero failures with the real failure count, and return `Status = "Fail"` whenever an allocation's `MatchCount <> 1`. Expose unmatched or ambiguous Name/Role keys and prevent them from silently disappearing through `ResPeriodAllocation-B`. Resolve them against the approved identity mapping before cluster analysis, or use an explicitly approved exclusion policy. This repairs the 2 October weakening; it does not imply that unmatched BD rows have already been found.

2. **Retain and complete the recent cluster repairs. Source: recent changes requiring verification, not a proven new defect.** Keep numeric chronological ordering, single-day start/end recognition, Resource-scoped fills and the Resource-plus-index lookup in `ResDaysAllocated`, `AllocationCode`, `ClusterIDStart`, `ClusterIDEnd` and `Clusters`. Sort each nested Resource table immediately before fill operations. Extend `A1ClusterLookup_CHECK` to validate paired dates, ordering, coverage and non-overlap, rather than just keys and equal boundary counts. Reverting to Unit2's lookup would restore a known cross-worker error.

3. **Repair the day-classification expressions. Source: original code.** In `WD Type`, replace the quoted third-day names compared with numbers by actual Resource-scoped neighbour values for the approved conditional rule. Harmonise category spellings with the priority table. In `ResDayPotentialAvailabilityTABLE`, replace the always-true exclusions with explicit decisions whose downstream meaning is tested. A simple global `or` to `and` replacement is insufficient: unallocated U rows are currently zeroed by A.2, while R rows can survive optional reductions.

4. **Choose cluster extensions jointly in A.1. Source: missing control in the original code.** Add an explicit planning stage before capacity and reduction targets. Original allocated work remains the baseline. Before accepting each next adjacent available day, consider both ends of its cluster and the facing end of its neighbour. Share the five-worked-day allowance across both ends. For an original gap of `g` full off days, facing extensions together may consume at most `g - 2`; admit none when only two off days remain. Recalculate allowances after each choice. Use existing preferences only among legal choices, with stable tie-breaks. Publish owning cluster, side, decision and exclusion reason.

5. **Separate worked-day detection from full-shift measures. Source: original counting risk; business definition needs confirmation.** `ResDaysAllocated` currently recognises a worked day only when an individual shift's allocation exceeds 0.7. Define a rest-rule work flag separately from that qualifying-shift measure so partial work cannot silently become a full day off. Preserve the source's distinction between worked-day count and elapsed cluster span. Specify treatment of existing one-day internal gaps, spare-only workers, growth beyond the original two-day window and roster-edge work; do not invent those policies through a Boolean repair or assume unseen boundary days are off.

6. **Make prepared eligible choices govern C#. Source: original assembly gap.** Separate shift choice from day eligibility to avoid a dependency cycle. Include eligible unallocated days with exactly one available shift in the preparation feeding `SingleShiftDayPeriods` and `C' ResSingleShiftDayTABLE`; the current allocation-only branch omits them. Calculate A.1's prepared capacity and cap targets from the eligible selected rows. In A.2 `ResPeriodCappedAvailability(C#)TABLE`, explicitly zero ineligible spare before applying optional reductions. Keep original availability as audit evidence, rather than letting an unselected raw row bypass the day decision.

7. **Repair the priority interfaces. Source: original code.** Preserve or explicitly rejoin `ClusterAllocation` before A.1 `ResPeriodCapPrioritised` expands it and A.2 consumes it; it is currently dropped in `ResDayPotentialAvailabilityTABLE`. Supply B `IMPORT ResPeriodNWDTABLE` with the actual non-working-day priority output from `ResPeriodShiftNWDTABLE`, rather than assuming the U-only `ResPeriodWDTABLE` worksheet provides it. Validate required columns and Resource/Period uniqueness. Confirm the approved saved table or sheet binding before changing navigation; an M definition alone does not prove the loaded interface.

8. **Select reductions using jointly accepted state. Source: original code.** Update A.2 `Period Running Total`/`Resource Running total`, B `ReDistributeResAvailability`/`ReDistribPeriodAvailbility TABLE`, and B `SubtractOverallatedPeriods`/`SubtractOverallocatedResources`. Spend period and Resource allowances only when a reduction satisfies both limits. Reconsider later candidates when an earlier one is rejected. Define partial-boundary handling for fractional measures. Retain the newer arithmetic helper: its equivalence to the old prefixes is not the defect.

9. **Enforce the cap using valid spare. Source: original cap gap carried into recent contract-cap integration.** In A.1/B contract checks, validate the imported cap against the Settings maximum as well as its positive whole-shift value. Keep A.1's early target and B's Resource-level cap calculations, then reconcile the actual retained result to that cap. B's extra cap reductions must not depend on period demand surplus. Rank feasible spare removals by greatest surplus, then smallest resulting shortfall, then approved preferences and stable ties. Recalculate coverage after each accepted removal. Preserve the agreed extension structure when trimming; removing outermost extension days or choosing shorter prefix/suffix lengths are options to assess. Do not add a clean-up priority for spare already breaking the rest rule: that spare must have been excluded upstream. Preserve allocation facts and separately report pre-existing allocation breaches. Count overlaps once.

10. **Complete publication checks and repair validation coverage. Source: original missing checks and omissions in recent validation.** Gate C# on eligible spare and its combined planned calendar; gate C### on the prepared final rows, effective cap, eligibility lineage and the same approved rest rules. Checks must read staging rows rather than their own published output. Update the test target and stale final-gate assertion in `scripts/test-capacity-distrib-b.mjs`. The earlier helper and join fixtures proved specific arithmetic/interfaces, not worker-calendar compliance. Add complete-chain cases for competing end extensions, gaps of two/three/four days, five-day clusters, single-shift availability, partial work, unmatched names, demand shortages, caps below 20, spare-only workers and row-order changes.

## Acceptance conditions and remaining decisions

C# must already contain no spare that violates the approved cluster/break rule. B must respect that eligible set and publish no spare-induced violation or unexplained cap breach. Existing allocations are not deleted to disguise a conflict. For a three-day external gap, accepting one facing extension must prevent the opposing extension from consuming a second day; an original five-worked-day group must have no remaining extension allowance.

The present cap applies over the Settings roster. Confirm whether the maximum of 20 also has a separate calendar-month scope. Count definitions, conditional internal gaps, spare-only cluster construction and boundary coverage require explicit business rules before implementation. BD's distribution files import `Effective Shift Cap`; its upstream calculation still requires the requested exact BD StaffListMaster extract.

The recorded source comparisons and decision traces support this proposal. A current native M probe could not start its configured engine; complete M and authorised Excel acceptance checks remain pending. An affected Resource/date example is also needed to distinguish the actual BD trigger from the confirmed source risks.
